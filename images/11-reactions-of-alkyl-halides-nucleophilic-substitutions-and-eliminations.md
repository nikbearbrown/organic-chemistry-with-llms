## Figure 11.8 — SN1 mechanism, two steps with energy diagram

**Placement:** §11.2, mechanism subsection.

**Brief:** Two-panel figure.

*Panel A:* the mechanism in arrow notation. Step 1: (CH₃)₃CBr → (CH₃)₃C⁺ + Br⁻ (slow), with curly arrow showing C–Br bond electrons departing onto bromide; the carbocation drawn explicitly as flat sp² with empty p-orbital. Step 2: (CH₃)₃C⁺ + H₂O → (CH₃)₃COH₂⁺ → (CH₃)₃COH + H⁺ (fast), with curly arrows for water lone pair attack and proton departure.

*Panel B:* an energy profile (free energy on y-axis, reaction coordinate on x-axis). Two transition states with an intermediate valley between them (the carbocation). The first TS is higher than the second — that's why step 1 is rate-determining. Reactants and products labeled.

Caption: *The two-step SN1 mechanism. Slow dissociation, fast capture, with a real carbocation intermediate sitting in an energy valley between the transition states.*

## Figure 11.13 — Anti vs. syn periplanar (Newman projections)

**Placement:** §11.3, anti-periplanar subsection.

**Brief:** Two Newman projections side by side, viewed down the C–C bond between the α and β carbons.

*Left (anti periplanar):* H at the top of the front carbon, X (Br) at the bottom of the back carbon, 180° apart. Substituents on each carbon in staggered positions.

*Right (syn periplanar):* H at the top of the front carbon, X at the top of the back carbon, 0° apart (eclipsed). Substituents on each carbon in eclipsed positions.

Energy comparison: anti is the lower-energy arrangement. Caption: *Both periplanar arrangements allow orbital overlap, but anti gives staggered geometry around the C–C bond and is preferred by ~5 kJ/mol of torsional strain.*

## Figure 11.14 — Trans-diaxial requirement on cyclohexane

**Placement:** §11.3, cyclohexane subsection.

**Brief:** Two cyclohexane chair conformations.

*Top:* a generic cyclohexane with H axial (β) and Cl axial (α) on adjacent carbons, both pointing away from the ring on opposite faces — trans-diaxial. Arrow indicating "E2 fires" with the alkene product drawn alongside.

*Bottom:* the same molecule with H equatorial and Cl equatorial. Arrow with "E2 forbidden" struck through. (Or: H axial, Cl equatorial; same forbidden result.)

Caption: *On a cyclohexane, the H and the leaving group must both be axial and on opposite faces of the ring. Equatorial substituents cannot eliminate.*

## Figure 11.15 — Menthyl vs. neomenthyl chloride

**Placement:** §11.3, after the cyclohexane figure.

**Brief:** Two chair structures shown side by side.

*Left — neomenthyl chloride:* methyl and isopropyl groups equatorial, chlorine axial. Adjacent β-H also axial. Trans-diaxial achieved. E2 product: 3-menthene (the more substituted alkene). Reaction rate: 1 (relative).

*Right — menthyl chloride:* methyl, isopropyl, and chlorine all equatorial in the more stable chair. Must ring-flip to a higher-energy chair (all axial) before E2 can occur. The only available trans-diaxial H gives 2-menthene (the less substituted alkene). Reaction rate: 1/200.

Caption: *Same atoms, different stereochemistry, 200-fold rate difference, and a complete switch in product. The chemistry is controlled by the conformation.*

Caption: *E1 shares the rate-determining step with SN1. The carbocation, once formed, partitions between substitution and elimination.*

## Figure 11.17 — E1cB mechanism

**Placement:** §11.3, E1cB subsection.

**Brief:** Two-step mechanism with carbanion intermediate.

*Step 1:* base abstracts an acidic β-H (α to a carbonyl). Show the resulting carbanion stabilized by resonance with the carbonyl — draw both resonance forms (C⁻–C=O ↔ C=C–O⁻).

*Step 2:* the carbanion expels the leaving group on the *adjacent* carbon (one carbon away from the carbanion). Arrow shows the carbanion's lone pair forming the C=C bond and the leaving group departing.

Use a 3-hydroxybutyryl thioester as the substrate (biological example): HOCH(CH₃)–CH₂–C(=O)–SR. Product: crotonyl thioester (CH₃–CH=CH–C(=O)–SR).

Caption: *E1cB: C–H breaks first, giving a resonance-stabilized carbanion; the leaving group departs in a second step. Common in biological dehydrations.*

## Figure 11.18 — Mechanism decision tree

**Placement:** §11.4, summary section.

**Brief:** A flow diagram. Top: "Alkyl halide + Nu/Base → ?". Three branches by substrate:

*Primary:* → if good Nu → SN2; → if bulky strong base → E2; → if α-H to C=O → E1cB.

*Secondary:* → if good Nu, polar aprotic → SN2; → if strong base → E2 (with some SN2); → if weak Nu, protic, allylic/benzylic → SN1 + E1; → if α-H to C=O → E1cB.

*Tertiary:* → if base → E2; → if neutral protic, no base → SN1 + E1; → if α-H to C=O → E1cB.

Color-code each mechanism box (e.g., SN2 blue, SN1 green, E2 orange, E1 red, E1cB purple) and reuse the colors throughout the chapter so students can match by sight. Caption: *The decision rule, by substrate class.*
