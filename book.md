## Premise

An organic chemistry textbook that teaches the chemistry *and* teaches the student to use LLMs honestly while learning it. Two threads, braided. The chemistry thread does the work of any good orgo book: structure, bonding, mechanism, the patient accumulation of why-this-attacks-that. The LLM thread runs alongside — what does Claude get right when you ask it to predict a product, where does it confabulate a mechanism, what happens when you ask it to interpret an NMR and the chemical shifts don't quite match.

The book treats LLMs the way a good lab manual treats a balance: as an instrument with known precision, known failure modes, and earned trust. Useful for translating jargon, for stress-testing your understanding, for generating practice problems. Unreliable for novel mechanism prediction, for stereochemistry it hasn't seen, for synthesis routes that demand judgment about scale or cost. The book teaches both halves.

## Voice notes

Inherits the workshop's Feynman register. Organic-chemistry-specific adjustments:

- **Mechanism is the substance.** Names of reactions are mnemonics; mechanisms are the actual chemistry. Every reaction the book teaches gets unpacked at the level of arrows, partial charges, and *why this attacks here* — not "the SN2 reaction proceeds via..." but the specific electron movement, the geometry, the reason the leaving group leaves.
- **Stereochemistry is not optional.** A chapter that handwaves stereo is a chapter that cheats. When stereochemistry matters to the outcome, the book draws it, names it, and shows where models will fail to track it.
- **Spectroscopy is reasoning, not lookup.** NMR and IR chapters teach the student to read a spectrum the way a detective reads a crime scene — what does this signal *require*, what does it *rule out*. LLMs are particularly weak here; the LLM thread in spectroscopy chapters earns its keep by surfacing those failures.
- **The LLM thread is examined, not evangelized.** When a chapter asks the student to consult a model, it asks them to verify against a textbook mechanism, to draw the arrows themselves, to check whether the model's confident product is actually the kinetic vs. thermodynamic outcome the question demands.
